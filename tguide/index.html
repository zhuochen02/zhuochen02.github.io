Key Insight

With only four denoising steps, features from an earlier step can be far from the target state. TGuide uses the previous video chunk’s features at the same timestep as a complementary reference, then adapts them to the current chunk to replace selected full-model evaluations.

Method

ATC predicts the current chunk’s hidden state in three steps: Anchor estimates its denoising evolution, Transport aligns historical features, and Correct uses those features to refine the prediction.

Inputs and notation

At denoising step iii of video chunk jjj, ATC uses two cached hidden states. A hidden state is the diffusion transformer’s internal feature representation, before its output head converts it into a denoising velocity.

The predictor also receives xembx_{\mathrm{emb}}xemb​, the current noisy latent after the input head, and C\mathcal CC, the generation conditions and historical key–value (KV) context.

Anchor

Starting from aaa, a lightweight predictor uses the current noisy tokens xembx_{\mathrm{emb}}xemb​ and context C\mathcal CC to estimate the change needed to reach the target denoising stage. Adding this change to aaa gives the initial prediction h^A\hat h_Ah^A​.

h^A=a+ΔhD(xemb,a,C)\hat h_A = a + \Delta h_D\big(x_{\mathrm{emb}},a,\mathcal C\big)h^A​=a+ΔhD​(xemb​,a,C)

ΔhD\Delta h_DΔhD​ is the learned denoising increment. The resulting h^A\hat h_Ah^A​ will be refined in Correct.

Transport

The current noisy tokens query the previous chunk’s reference ppp through cross-attention. Retrieving across spatial positions accounts for content displaced by motion and produces T(p)\mathcal T(p)T(p), history aligned to the current tokens for use in Correct.

T(p)=CrossAttn ⁣(Q(xemb),K(p),V(p))\mathcal T(p)=\mathrm{CrossAttn}\!\big(Q(x_{\mathrm{emb}}),K(p),V(p)\big)T(p)=CrossAttn(Q(xemb​),K(p),V(p))

QQQ projects current tokens into queries; KKK and VVV project historical features into keys and values. T(p)\mathcal T(p)T(p) is the retrieved historical state.

Correct

A lightweight network combines T(p)\mathcal T(p)T(p) with xembx_{\mathrm{emb}}xemb​ and aaa to predict a correction. The learned gate ggg controls its strength separately for each token. Adding the gated correction to h^A\hat h_Ah^A​ produces the final hidden state h^\hat hh^.

h^=h^A+g⊙ΔhC(xemb,a,T(p))\hat h=\hat h_A+g\odot\Delta h_C\big(x_{\mathrm{emb}},a,\mathcal T(p)\big)h^=h^A​+g⊙ΔhC​(xemb​,a,T(p))

ΔhC\Delta h_CΔhC​ predicts the correction; ⊙\odot⊙ applies each token’s scalar gate across its feature channels. A small gate keeps the result close to h^A\hat h_Ah^A​.

How is the gate computed?g=σ ⁣(G(xemb,a,T(p)))g=\sigma\!\big(G(x_{\mathrm{emb}},a,\mathcal T(p))\big)g=σ(G(xemb​,a,T(p)))

GGG is a learned gating network and σ\sigmaσ is the sigmoid function, which bounds each gate between 0 and 1.

Training

Offline regression first teaches ATC to approximate the frozen generator. Self-rollout on-policy distillation then trains it on states shaped by its own predictions, reducing the training–inference gap across denoising steps and video chunks. On Self Forcing, ATC costs 5.3% of a full DiT pass’s FLOPs.
